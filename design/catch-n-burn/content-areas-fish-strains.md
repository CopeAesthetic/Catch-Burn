# Catch n' Burn — Areas, Ponds, Fish & Strains (Content Draft v0.2)

Source of truth for regional content. Built from `docs/research/catch-n-burn-research.md`.

**Rules used**
- 8 areas in difficulty order. Each has 4 water bodies ("ponds"), each pond has 10 fish and 4 strains.
- Fish rarity per pond: 3 Common · 2 Uncommon · 2 Rare · 1 Epic · 1 Legendary · 1 Lost Legend.
- Fish keep their **real names** (players learn real fish). Ponds and strains get the **pun names**; the real name is kept beside them for reference and for the in-game "Unc" log.
- Water bodies are real, popular, region-defining lakes/rivers (Area 1 Pond 1 is the fictional Home Pond, modeled on a Maine sporting-camp pond).
- Strains: Reggie / Home Grown / Top Shelf are based on real strains that sell in that region (2025 Headset / Marijuana Herald / Leafly data where it exists; widely sold national strains where a state has no data). **Straight Fire strains are fictional** on purpose — they are the hidden mythic tier.
- **Lost Legends** are real extinct, near-extinct or record/folklore fish tied to that exact water. They are photographed and released, never sold (owner decision 2026-11-15): the reward is the pond's Straight Fire strain plus a trophy photo in Unc's log.
- Some species repeat between ponds in the same area where that is what real anglers catch; the Content team can swap duplicates during `/design-system`.
- Accuracy level: game-accurate, from state fishery surveys and DNR species lists. Verify any line before using it in marketing.
- Sensitivity: pun on English words only; keep Hawaiian (with ʻokina) and Spanish names spelled correctly. The Lost River Sucker (C'waam) is culturally significant to the Klamath Tribes — no jokes on that entry.
- **Real people (owner decision 2026-11-15):** three real names stay as respectful tributes, each with a true story in the "Unc's stories" section at the end of this file — Jack Herer (Jack Herring), Jenny Kush (Jenny Kush Jig) and Les Anderson (Les Anderson's King). No jokes at their expense. No living real people: Gary Payton Pole was swapped out.
- **Brands (owner decision 2026-11-15):** names that leaned on a real trademark were replaced. The old → new list is in the "Rename log" at the end of this file.

## Unc's stories (draft v0.1)

True stories John (Unc) tells from the fishing log. A story unlocks the first time you buy the strain or land the fish it belongs to. These three are the tribute entries: real people, told straight, no jokes at their expense. **Verify every fact against a primary source before it ships or is used in marketing.**

### Jack Herring — for Jack Herer
*Unlocks with the Reggie strain at The Colum-BUD-ia River.*

- Jack Herer (1939–2010) was an American cannabis and hemp activist, often called "the Emperor of Hemp".
- He wrote *The Emperor Wears No Clothes* (1985), a book arguing for hemp and cannabis that stayed in print for decades.
- The strain that carries his name was bred in the Netherlands in the 1990s and named in his honour.
- He died in Eugene, Oregon, in 2010 — which is why his story sits on the Oregon river.

Unc's voice (draft): "Fella spent his whole life tellin' folks a plant could be rope, paper and a good afternoon. They named one after him. Seems fair."

### Jenny Kush Jig — for Jenny Kush
*Unlocks with the Reggie strain at Lake WEED.*

- "Jenny Kush" was the name Jenny Monson went by: a Colorado cannabis activist and mother.
- She was killed by a drunk driver in 2013.
- Breeders named the strain in her memory.

Unc's voice (draft): "Never met her. Folks who did say she fought for people and was kind doin' it. That's worth a name on a jar."

Note for the writer: this entry is a memorial. Keep it short and warm; no drinking jokes anywhere near it.

### Les Anderson's King — for Les Anderson
*Unlocks when you land the Lost Legend at The Kenai Smell That? River.*

- On May 17, 1985, Les Anderson of Soldotna, Alaska, landed a 97-pound 4-ounce king (Chinook) salmon on the Kenai River.
- It still stands as the world record for a sport-caught king salmon.
- Anderson, a local car dealer, died in 2003; a mount of the fish is displayed in Soldotna.

Unc's voice (draft): "Ninety-seven pounds, four ounces. He fought it from the boat and finished it on the bank. Nobody's beat it since. You get to take its picture — that's plenty."
